# How to spot a good clip (and a bad one)

Use this before you spend any time editing. The clip you pick matters more than the edit:
the same channel, with the same editing, got 26M on one video and 60K on another.

---

## Step 1: The 5 must-haves
Watch the clip once with **the sound off**, then once with sound. Tick each one.

- [ ] **1. Something actually happens.** A fail, a surprise, a clever move, a funny line or an emotional moment.
      "Guys doing something" doesn't count, and "something cool" doesn't count.
- [ ] **2. You can say the joke in one sentence.** "The mortar flips over when it fires." If you can't say it simply, viewers won't get it.
- [ ] **3. You understand it with the sound off.** Most people scroll muted. If the joke is only spoken, it needs captions to work.
- [ ] **4. You can see it clearly.** It's close enough, bright enough and sharp enough to see what happens on a phone screen.
- [ ] **5. The good part is in the first ~5 seconds,** or you can cut the clip so it is.

**Missing #1? Skip it.** Nothing else matters.
**Missing 2 or more of the rest? Skip it.**

---

## Step 2: Instant red flags (skip, or know the risk)
- [ ] **A TikTok / Instagram logo or someone else's text baked into the middle of the video.**
      A logo in a corner can sometimes be cropped out. Text across the middle usually can't.
- [ ] **A long, boring start** (5+ seconds of waiting) with no way to cut it.
- [ ] **Very dark, very far away, or very blurry,** so the action is tiny.
- [ ] **Real injuries, deaths or real combat casualties.** Never.
- [ ] **A clip you've already seen go viral** many times. People will scroll past.
- [ ] **The whole joke is a slur or a cheap shot.** It limits reach, even when censored.

---

## Step 3: Bonus points (what turns good into great)
- [ ] **Real danger or stakes** (a weapon, an explosion, height, an angry NCO). Viewers wince, then laugh.
- [ ] **The fail lands fast**, 1–3 seconds in after you cut it.
- [ ] **A clear reaction afterwards** (someone hits the dirt, laughs, or acts like nothing happened).
- [ ] **People will argue in the comments** ("whose fault was that?", "that's the Army for you").
- [ ] **Anyone gets it**, not just soldiers.
- [ ] **You haven't seen this exact thing before.**

**4 or more bonus points = breakout candidate.** 2–3 = solid. 0–1 = it'll be average at best.

---

## Step 4: Which type is it?
**Your own channel's results come first.** They beat any competitor data.

**Proven on YOUR channel:**
- **Reunion / emotional surprise:** about **100K** on a new channel with only a few videos. Everyone gets it, it hits hard and people share it.
  Competitors agree: Combatful's biggest videos are reunions (72.9M, 54.6M, 31.4M).
- **Funny skit (staged comedy):** **40K+**.

**From competitor data** (not tested on your channel yet), roughly best first:
1. **Danger → fail.** Something dangerous goes wrong (the mortar flip).
2. **Something real breaks into the game** (in airsoft clips: the homeowner thinks the airsoft gun is real).
3. **A good-sport moment.**
4. **A clever move / skill.**
5. **Relatable military humour** (complaining about the Army). Solid, rarely huge.
6. **Animals + soldiers.** Cute, broad appeal, rarely huge.

**If it doesn't fit any of these types, that's a warning sign.**

**What makes a reunion clip good:**
- [ ] The person being surprised **doesn't know**, and you can see that in the first 2 seconds.
- [ ] You can **see their face** when they realise.
- [ ] **A real reaction:** tears, running, a big hug or falling to their knees. A small smile isn't enough.
- [ ] A **fake problem or misdirect** before the reveal works even better (Combatful's "missing diploma" got 72.9M).
- [ ] **About 20–45 seconds:** the build-up needs time, but cut every slow part.

**What makes a skit good:**
- [ ] The joke is clear within **2–3 seconds**.
- [ ] **One idea**, with a twist at the end.
- [ ] Relatable to soldiers **and** understandable to civilians.

## Your 4 editing habits to fix (from your 100K and 40K videos)
1. **Captions in 2–4 word chunks**, never one word at a time.
2. **Never explain what's about to happen.** A voiceover goes after the payoff.
3. **Let the best moment run 2–3 seconds** (the reunion hug lasted under 1 second).
4. **Crop away other people's text** instead of blurring it.
5. **Keep the face in frame.** If the joke is someone's reaction, never cut to their chin.

---

## The 10-second version
1. **Did something happen?** No → skip.
2. **Can I see it on a phone with the sound off?** No → skip, or only post it if the audio joke is great.
3. **Can I cut it so it starts within ~2 seconds of the action?** No → skip.
4. **Is there a watermark or someone else's text in the middle?** Yes → skip.
5. **Would I send this to a friend?** If you hesitate, it's average.
