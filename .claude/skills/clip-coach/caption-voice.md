## 2. What makes these read as human

1. **Most captions are boring on purpose.** About 70% are flat, literal, present-tense actions with no subject:
   `*THROWS GRENADE*`, `*AIMS*`, `*SHOOTS*`, `*WAITING..*`. That flatness is the setup. Only **1–2 captions per
   video carry attitude**, and they sit on the payoff and the reaction. If every line is a joke, the jokes cancel out
   and it reads like an ad.
2. **The joke is the character's thought, not the editor's commentary.** `*SH*T WRONG GUN..*`, `*THINKS ABOUT IT..*`,
   `*MAYBE NEXT TIME*`: written as what the soldier (or the machine) is thinking, in their own casual words.
   The editor never "presents" the joke.
3. **Deadpan beats clever.** The funniest lines are the dumbest-sounding: `*IT'S A WALL..*`, `DAMN..`,
   `*ENEMY IS COOKED..*`. No wordplay and no internet catchphrases.
4. **Sarcasm and understatement:** say less than the moment deserves (`*MAYBE NEXT TIME*` after almost shooting
   down an airliner), or the opposite of what happened (`*ENEMY IS COOKED..*` after a toy-gun pop).
5. **Raw speech, typed fast:** `WTF`, `DAMN`, `SH*T` (self-censored), `NOO-`, a cut-off `WAS THAT-`. It looks like a
   friend typed it while watching.
6. **Imperfection is left in:** `DISSAPOINTED`, `LOCKING AT TARGET`, "Payed" in a title. Nobody proofread it,
   which reads as a real person. (Don't fake typos on purpose every time. Just don't polish.)
7. **Counters and repetition:** `X2`, `X3`, `ONE` `TWO` `THREE`. Narrating like someone pointing at the screen.
8. **Punctuation:** two dots `..` (not three, not `!`) on suspense and reactions. Asterisks = action or thought.
   Plain text without asterisks = spoken words.
9. **Tags are plain roles, never names:** `SOLDIER`, `AIRSOFTER`, `RANDOM GUY`, `CAMERAMAN`, `POV-SOLDIER`, `C-RAM`,
   and even `GRENADE:`. "Random guy" is itself casual and funny.
10. **Specific nouns in the setup:** `*AIMS AT LAKE*`, `*CIVILIAN PLANE*`. One concrete detail makes the fail land harder.

## 3. Caption types and when to use them

| Type | Pattern | Where | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Literal action | `*VERB(S) OBJECT*`, 1–3 words | Hook + build (most lines) | `*THROWS GRENADE*` |
| Specific setup | `*VERB AT [CONCRETE THING]*` | First caption | `*AIMS AT LAKE*` |
| Stakes label | `*TWO WORDS*` (cyan, no tag) | Second 1–2 | `*CIVILIAN PLANE*` |
| Waiting | `*WAITING..*` | Just before the payoff | `*WAITING..*` |
| Inner thought | `ROLE: *casual first-person thought..*` | **Payoff** (red) | `*SH*T WRONG GUN..*` |
| Deadpan obvious | `*IT'S A [THING]..*` | Payoff | `*IT'S A WALL..*` |
| Raw speech | `WTF` / `NOO- X2` / `WAS THAT-` | Payoff + reaction | `NO! X3` |
| Sarcastic verdict | `*[NOUN] IS COOKED..*` / `*MAYBE NEXT TIME*` | **Last caption** | `*ENEMY IS COOKED..*` |
| Understated reaction | `ROLE: DAMN..` | Last caption | `DAMN..` |
| Emotion label (story) | `*ONE STATE*` | Story build | `*DISSAPOINTED*`, `*TRYING NOT TO LAUGH*` |
| Event label (story) | `*TWO-WORD EVENT*` | Story payoff | `*REUNION HUG*`, `*VICTORY WALK*` |

## 3b. Spoken words, swearing and slurs
- **Spoken lines are captioned as plain CAPS without asterisks**, word-synced, split into 2–4 word cards
  (`A IS FOR ARMY`, `R IS FOR-`). Asterisks stay reserved for actions and thoughts.
- Caption exactly what is said. Take the wording from `transcribe.py`, not from memory or guesswork.
- **Slurs:** caption what he actually says, censored with a symbol (`R is for ret*rd`), never spelled out in full.
  In the audio, mute the word and put a meme hit on it (the user's choice: `vine_boom.mp3`) instead of a 1 kHz beep.
  Reveal it in two steps (`R is for-`, then `R is for ret*rd` on the word) so the caption doesn't spoil it.
  Keep slurs out of the title. (ArmoredPOVs' "When Soldier Says The R Word.." got 1.28M, well below their hits.)
- The f-word: bleep it in the audio and write it as `F*CK` in the caption. That's how competitors self-censor (`SH*T`).
- When the speaker is uncertain, use a plain role tag (`SOLDIER:`, `CAMERAMAN:`, `RANDOM GUY:`) and ask the user who said it.

## 4. Rules for writing captions

- Write about 70% literal, 30% attitude. Attitude goes only on the payoff and the last caption.
- Use their vocabulary: cooked, damn, wtf, bro, nah, sh*t (censored), "random guy", "lowkey". Avoid
  "full send", "speedrun", "unlocked", "new PR", "W", "main character", "NPC" and anything that sounds like a brand or a trend list.
- Keep it to 1–4 words per caption. Five is the maximum.
- Write the punchline as the character's own thought, not your comment about them.
- End on a flat line with `..`.
- Read every caption out loud in a bored voice. If it sounds like a copywriter wrote it, rewrite it dumber.
